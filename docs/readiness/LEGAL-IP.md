## Brand, copyright and ownership

- The owner has fixed **Ternuxx** as the intended product/repository brand. This audit does not claim that the word mark is registered, available, or clear in Bangladesh or other markets.
- Copyright and trademark are different. Copyright protects qualifying expression such as original code, text, images and logo artwork. The product name/word mark is a brand-clearance and trademark question; a `©` notice does not reserve a product name. WIPO explains copyright as protection of expression rather than names or ideas ([WIPO copyright FAQ](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/faq-copyright)); Bangladesh-specific outcomes need counsel.
- The root LICENSE uses `Copyright (C) 2026 Sobuj Miah` and GPL-3.0-or-later for original project material within owner control. This is not a trademark registration or proof that every contribution is owned by one person. Review provenance and third-party contributions file by file; do not extend the project license to materials with separate terms.
- Logo/source-file ownership, fonts, icon sets, screenshots, generated artwork, photographs and website copy need a dated asset ledger with author, source, license, commercial-use/redistribution terms, and attribution.
- Git author metadata has three commits attributed to Sobuj Miah and two generated by GitHub Actions. That is useful evidence, not conclusive assignment evidence for every line or asset.

## License model options for counsel/owner

| Option | Commercial effect | Advantages | Risks / owner trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recognized permissive open-source license (e.g. Apache-2.0/MIT) | Commercial use and redistribution generally permitted subject to terms | Low friction, commercial service revenue remains possible, Apache-2.0 includes express patent terms | Others can sell/redistribute forks; product differentiation relies on service, trust, quality and trademark. Does not preserve noncommercial-only restrictions. |
| Recognized copyleft open-source license (e.g. GPL-3.0) | Commercial use is still allowed; distribution obligations apply to covered derivative/combined works | Stronger reciprocity for distributed modifications, community compatibility | Does not prohibit commercial competitors; integration boundary/derivative-work questions need counsel; customer compliance burden can be higher. |
| Proprietary/proprietary-commercial license | Use, redistribution and modification restricted by the written grant | More direct control over code use and commercial licensing | Reduces adoption/contributions; drafting/enforcement costs; “source available” is not open source; a website's public code remains usable only under express permission. |
| Noncommercial/custom addendum | Attempts to restrict commercial use by recipients | Aligns with an owner wish to reserve commercial permissions | Not OSI open source; ambiguity over what is commercial, forks, services, business users and exceptions. The historic PolyForm/addendum wording makes this especially counsel-dependent. |

Do not choose or implement any option in this draft. OSI states that all OSI-approved open-source licenses permit commercial use ([OSI FAQ](https://opensource.org/faq)); if the owner's central goal is to prevent others from commercializing the code, the project must not market that license as open source. Brand/trademark protection and code licensing should be designed together but remain separate.

## International readiness after Bangladesh

Before targeting users in another country, identify the jurisdictions from marketing, sales, data-subject location, cloud processors and support. At a minimum, review local consumer terms/refunds, privacy/data-transfer rules, sanctions/export controls where relevant, payment/withholding, software patent/copyright and trademark clearance. Do not publish a one-size-fits-all global terms page before that review. U.S. Copyright Office and WIPO sources are conceptual background only; Bangladesh counsel controls local advice.
